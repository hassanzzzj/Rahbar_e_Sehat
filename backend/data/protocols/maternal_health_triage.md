# Maternal Health and Pregnancy Triage

## Obstetric Emergencies

### Emergency (EMERGENCY)
- Vaginal bleeding in pregnancy (any trimester)
  - First trimester: suspect ectopic or miscarriage
  - Third trimester: suspect placenta previa or abruption
- Severe abdominal pain with vaginal bleeding
- Preterm labor (<37 weeks) with regular contractions
- Rupture of membranes before 37 weeks
- Severe pre-eclampsia signs:
  - BP >160/110 mmHg
  - Severe headache, visual disturbances
  - Epigastric pain
  - Oliguria
- Eclampsia (seizures in pregnancy)
- Decreased fetal movements >24 hours

**Immediate Actions:**
- Left lateral position
- IV access
- Monitor fetal heart rate
- Urgent obstetric consultation

**Referral:** DHQ or tertiary hospital with obstetric unit and NICU

## Postpartum Emergencies

### Emergency (EMERGENCY)
- Postpartum hemorrhage (>500ml vaginal, >1000ml cesarean)
- Fever >38°C within 24 hours of delivery
- Foul-smelling lochia
- Severe perineal pain with swelling
- Signs of sepsis (fever, tachycardia, hypotension)

**Referral:** DHQ with blood bank and surgical capability

## Routine Antenatal Concerns

### Urgent (URGENT)
- Mild pre-eclampsia (BP 140-159/90-109 mmHg)
- Gestational diabetes requiring insulin adjustment
- Anemia (Hb <8 g/dL)
- Urinary tract infection symptoms

**Referral:** THQ obstetric clinic

### Routine (ROUTINE)
- Routine antenatal checkup
- Mild anemia (Hb 8-10 g/dL)
- Mild edema without hypertension
- Back pain, heartburn, constipation

**Referral:** BHU or RHC antenatal clinic

### Self-Care (SELF_CARE)
- Normal pregnancy discomforts
- Mild nausea/vomiting (hyperemesis excluded)
- Leg cramps
- Mild back pain

**Advice:** Rest, hydration, folic acid and iron supplementation, danger signs education

## Important Considerations
- ANY pregnant woman with abdominal pain + bleeding = EMERGENCY until proven otherwise
- Do NOT delay referral for investigations in suspected ectopic pregnancy
- Pre-eclampsia can progress rapidly — err on the side of urgent referral
- Cultural sensitivity: ensure female healthcare provider when possible
