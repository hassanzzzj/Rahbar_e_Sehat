# Emergency Triage Protocols

## Cardiac Emergencies

### Acute Chest Pain
**Urgency: EMERGENCY**
- Chest pain radiating to left arm, jaw, or back
- Associated diaphoresis (sweating), nausea, shortness of breath
- Pain lasting >15 minutes
- History of cardiac disease or risk factors (hypertension, diabetes, smoking)

**Immediate Actions:**
- Activate emergency services (Rescue 1122)
- Administer aspirin 300mg chewed (if not contraindicated)
- Monitor vital signs
- Prepare for CPR if cardiac arrest

**Referral:** Tertiary care hospital with cardiac catheterization lab

### Stroke Symptoms
**Urgency: EMERGENCY**
- Sudden onset facial drooping, arm weakness, speech difficulty (FAST)
- Sudden severe headache
- Sudden vision changes or loss of balance
- Time since onset <4.5 hours (thrombolysis window)

**Immediate Actions:**
- Activate emergency services immediately
- Note exact time of symptom onset
- CT head (non-contrast) at nearest facility
- Blood glucose check (rule out hypoglycemia)

**Referral:** DHQ or tertiary hospital with stroke unit

## Respiratory Emergencies

### Severe Asthma Exacerbation
**Urgency: EMERGENCY**
- Severe breathlessness at rest, unable to speak in sentences
- Peak flow <50% predicted or personal best
- Oxygen saturation <92%
- Use of accessory muscles, silent chest

**Immediate Actions:**
- High-flow oxygen
- Salbutamol nebulization 5mg
- Ipratropium bromide 500mcg nebulized
- Hydrocortisone 200mg IV

**Referral:** Emergency department

### Anaphylaxis
**Urgency: EMERGENCY**
- Sudden onset after allergen exposure
- Airway compromise (stridor, hoarseness)
- Hypotension (systolic <90 mmHg)
- Widespread urticaria, angioedema

**Immediate Actions:**
- Adrenaline 0.5mg IM (1:1000)
- High-flow oxygen
- IV access and fluid resuscitation
- Antihistamines and corticosteroids

**Referral:** Emergency department with ICU capability

## Trauma

### Major Trauma
**Urgency: EMERGENCY**
- Mechanism: high-speed RTA, fall >3m, penetrating injury
- GCS <13
- Systolic BP <90 mmHg
- Respiratory rate >30 or <10
- Obvious major hemorrhage

**Immediate Actions:**
- Activate trauma team
- ABCDE assessment
- Control hemorrhage (direct pressure, tourniquet if limb)
- IV access (2 large-bore cannulae)
- Tranexamic acid 1g IV within 3 hours

**Referral:** DHQ or tertiary trauma center
